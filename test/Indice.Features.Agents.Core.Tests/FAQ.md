## Question: What is Indice IAM and how is it delivered?
Answer: Indice IAM is a complete, MIT-licensed identity & access management product delivered as composable .NET libraries built on `Indice.Platform`. The client deploys it on their own infrastructure — their databases, DNS, TLS, and operations. It builds on standard foundations: ASP.NET Identity for the user store and Duende IdentityServer for OIDC/OAuth2.

## Question: Is Indice IAM a SaaS, and who controls the data?
Answer: No — it is a deployed system, not a SaaS. There is no SaaS layer in the request path. User data lives in your database in the region/cloud of your choice (on-prem, sovereign cloud, any cloud, or hybrid), and token-signing key material is your own PFX in your own key vault — it never leaves your environment. This is what makes it suitable for regulated finance, public-sector, and healthcare deployments.

## Question: Which feature libraries make up the product?
Answer: The product is composed of `Indice.Features.Identity.*` libraries: **Core** (users, devices, MFA, custom grants, validation pipeline, events), **Server** (management, device, MFA, TOTP, AppSettings runtime APIs), **SignInLogs** (async, enriched auth audit), **ActivityLogs** (entity-mutation audit), **UI** (Razor RCL with Bootstrap5/Tailwind), and **AdminUI** (Angular 20.3 operator SPA). Your ASP.NET Core host project sits on top and wires them together.

## Question: What is the difference between the IdentityPages template and the product?
Answer: The libraries are the product; the `IdentityPages` template is an optional `dotnet new` reference scaffold that demonstrates a complete production wiring. What the client integrates is the libraries. After integration, the deployed ASP.NET Core application *is* Indice IAM — running in their tenancy, under their brand, with their data.

## Question: Which identity surfaces does one deployment cover?
Answer: All four that most organisations end up running: **Workforce** (internal apps, role-based access), **B2B** (partner portals, federated logins, API consumers), **B2C** (public registration, social logins, branded journeys), and a **multi-tenant control plane** where the `ClientUser` entity binds operators to specific OIDC clients for B2B marketplace and white-label scenarios. One stack, one team, one upgrade path — all four share the same database, audit trail, and MFA/device-trust policies.

## Question: What authentication methods and external identity providers are supported?
Answer: Username + password (with an extended user store, password history, blacklist, and custom validators), magic-link/passwordless login, server-side sessions (revocable by row), and single-active-session enforcement. External providers include Apple, Google, Microsoft (tenant-aware), Facebook, GovGr, and any standard OIDC/OAuth2 IdP. Anti-enumeration protections rate-limit and redact user-lookup endpoints to prevent account discovery.

## Question: Which custom OAuth grants ship out of the box?
Answer: Four custom grant validators ship via the same `IExtensionGrantValidator` extension point Indice itself uses: **TOTP grant** (`urn:indice:totp`, for MFA step-up), **Device authentication** (`device_authentication`, for mobile/IoT), **Delegation grant** (`delegation`, for act-as/on-behalf-of back-office flows), and **OTP authenticate grant** (`otp_authenticate`, for passwordless OTP login).

## Question: What MFA methods and delivery channels are available?
Answer: Six methods across six delivery channels: push approval (via the SignalR hub at `/mfa`), FIDO2/WebAuthn (hardware keys and platform authenticators), TOTP (authenticator apps), SMS (country-aware via `CallingCodesProvider`), Email (fallback channel), and Viber (a regional channel for SE Europe). Delivery channels (`TotpDeliveryChannel`) are `Sms`, `Viber`, `PushNotification`, `Email`, `EToken`, and `None`. The per-user policy (`MfaPolicy`) can be `Optional`, `Encouraged`, `Enforced`, or `Prohibited`.

## Question: How does the MFA step-up flow work?
Answer: It is a two-stage, standard, debuggable pattern. `ExtendedResourceOwnerPasswordValidator` returns `needs_mfa` when two-factor is required; the client then calls `/token` again with the `urn:indice:totp` grant plus the OTP to complete authentication. In the UI login flow, a trusted-browser cookie can short-circuit MFA, while lockout policy guards repeated failures.

## Question: How does device trust and the activation delay work?
Answer: Devices are first-class entities — each has a row, a trust state, a push endpoint, and a lifecycle, managed via the device APIs (list/create/update/delete). Devices register untrusted and only elevate to trusted after a window (default 48h via `Devices.TrustActivationDelay`), so an attacker with phished credentials cannot immediately trust their own device. `RequirePasswordAfterUserUpdate` adds a second gate after profile changes, a per-user device cap (`MaxAllowedRegisteredDevices`) is runtime-updateable, and lifecycle events (`DeviceCreatedEvent`, `DeviceUpdatedEvent`, `DeviceTrustRequestedEvent`) let your SOC watch for anomalies.

## Question: Why are there two separate audit subsystems?
Answer: Sign-in logs are auth-shaped (one row per attempt); activity logs are entity-shaped (one row per mutation). **SignInLogs** captures authentication events through an async, enriched, geo-aware pipeline. **ActivityLogs** captures material entity mutations (user created, email/phone/password changed, client/role changes, device lifecycle) in its own database. Keeping them separate keeps both auditor and engineer queries fast, and lets you target tier-appropriate storage (hot for sign-in logs, cheaper long-retention storage for activity logs).

## Question: What is impossible-travel detection and how is it configured?
Answer: `ImpossibleTravelDetector` computes travel speed (km/h) between sign-in locations over a 14-day window, using geo enrichment that resolves IP to country and lat/lon (NetTopologySuite SRID 4326, the WGS84 standard). It runs off the auth hot path inside the queued SignInLogs pipeline. The response is configurable — either `PromptMfa` (step up) or `BlockLogin` (hard block) — and the feature is toggled via the `ImpossibleTravel` feature flag.

## Question: What is the identity validation pipeline and how do you extend it?
Answer: It is a chain-of-responsibility state machine that intercepts after authentication to enforce post-login requirements, each as a single-responsibility class: `RequiresTermsAcceptanceActivity`, `RequiresMfaOnboardingActivity`, `RequiresEmailVerificationActivity`, `RequiresPhoneNumberVerificationActivity`, and `RequiresPasswordChangeActivity`. Each activity decides whether to redirect (e.g. to `/accept-terms` or `/mfa-onboarding`) or pass. You extend it by implementing `IIdentityValidationActivity` and slotting your own step (cookie consent, KYC, custom claim collection) into the chain via DI ordering.

## Question: What security hardening ships by default?
Answer: Defaults intended to pass a security review: 20+ rate-limiting rules in the template's `appsettings.json` (per endpoint, per HTTP method); reCAPTCHA v3 invisible primary with automatic v2 checkbox fallback; lockout (configurable max failed attempts and duration); composable password validators including `NonCommonPasswordValidator` (blocks common-password lists) and `EmailDomainBlacklistValidator` (blocks disposable domains); anti-enumeration on username-exists and forgot-password endpoints; and token signing with your own PFX/key vault.

## Question: How does the reCAPTCHA v3-to-v2 fallback work and how is it configured?
Answer: reCAPTCHA v3 executes invisibly on form submit and is validated against Google's API for a risk score (0.0–1.0). If the score is below the configured `ScoreThreshold` (default 0.5), the v2 checkbox is shown as a fallback; the user completes it and resubmits, and both are validated server-side. It protects the login, register, and forgot-password pages. Configuration lives in the `Recaptcha` section (`SiteKey`, `SecretKey`, optional separate `SiteKeyV2`/`SecretKeyV2`, `ScoreThreshold`, `EnabledInLoginPage`), and the CSP must allow `https://www.google.com` and `https://www.gstatic.com` for `ConnectSrc`, `FrameSrc`, and `ScriptSrc`. Setting both keys to `null` (or omitting the section) disables it.

## Question: How do branding, theming, localization, and multi-tenancy work?
Answer: The UI is a Razor Class Library where every page is overrideable from the host project file-by-file (drop a file in to win, no fork). You can switch the entire UI framework between Bootstrap5 and Tailwind (full parallel page trees) via `[UIFramework]`. Per-client white-labelling uses `ClientThemeAttribute` + `ClientThemingService` + per-client `ClientThemeConfig`, and the `ClientUser` entity provides client-level RBAC (operators bound to specific OIDC clients). Localized resources ship for Greek, Spanish, Italian, Japanese, German, French, and Portuguese, with English as base; pre-built authorization policies (`BeAdmin`, `BeUsersReader`, `BeClientsWriter`) compose scope + role. One deployment can host many branded clients.

## Question: What is the AdminUI and how is it secured?
Answer: The AdminUI is an Angular 20.3 operator SPA mounted at `/admin`, built with ngx-charts (sign-in dashboards and geo visualisation), ngx-datatable, jsonforms (dynamic forms), and CKEditor (legal text). It manages users, roles, clients, API resources, scopes, sign-in logs, activity logs, and runtime settings. Critically, it is itself an OIDC client (`idsrv-admin-ui`) of the system it manages, with scoped access (`identity`, `identity:clients`, `identity:users`, `identity:logs`) — so operators sign in through the same MFA, device-trust, and sign-in-log flows as end users. It is themeable via `wwwroot/css/admin-ui-overrides.css`.

## Question: How is the system configured, and why so many databases?
Answer: Almost everything that can be a setting is a setting, organised into config sections: `IdentityOptions` (password, lockout, sign-in, claims, user, device policies), `IdentityServer` (endpoints, features, MFA, rate limiter, avatar, email, signing), `Recaptcha`, `Csp`, and delivery providers (`Email`/`Sms`/`Totp`/`PushNotifications`). `ConnectionStrings` defines five distinct databases — Identity, Configuration, Operational, SignInLogs, and ActivityLogs — to isolate blast radius and let you target tier-appropriate storage and SLAs. A runtime configuration API (`AppSettings`/`SettingHandlers`, enabled with `.AddDatabaseSettingEndpoints()`) lets operators tune password/MFA/phone/claim rules without a redeploy, with every change audited via ActivityLogs.

## Question: What password and lockout policy options are available?
Answer: The password policy is a composable rule set: `RequiredLength`, `RequireDigit`/`RequireUppercase`/`RequireLowercase`/`RequireNonAlphanumeric`, `RequiredUniqueChars`, `PasswordHistoryLimit` (defeats trivial rotation), `PasswordExpirationPolicy` (`Never`, `NextLogin`, `Monthly`, `Quarterly`, `Semesterly`, `Annually`, `Biannually`), a `Blacklist`, and `MaxAllowedUserNameSubset` (rejects passwords containing a slice of the username). Lockout options include `AllowedForNewUsers`, `MaxFailedAccessAttempts` (default 5), and `DefaultLockoutTimeSpan` (default 5 minutes) — strict enough to slow online attacks, lenient enough not to permanently lock out distracted users.

## Question: Where does Indice IAM shine compared with Amazon Cognito and Azure AD B2C?
Answer: On three axes especially. **Cost** — the software is free (MIT open source) with no per-MAU fees; you pay Indice for integration and support, and inactive users cost zero. **Sovereignty** — your database, your cloud, your key material, versus AWS-only or Azure-only with provider-managed keys. **Debuggability** — it's an open .NET stack you can step-through-debug (attach, set a breakpoint, reproduce a "cannot log in" report) rather than reading docs and filing a support ticket against a closed service. It also ships built-in capabilities others leave as DIY/custom: push-approval MFA, Viber OTP, device-trust with activation delay, the identity validation pipeline, impossible-travel detection, per-client theming, `ClientUser` RBAC, and a runtime configuration API.
